The same dashboard served merchants with completely different needs

A restaurant preparing to launch needed setup tasks, deadlines, installation status, and access to its Customer Success Manager. A live restaurant needed performance data and ways to expand its setup. Merchant Hub treated both the same.

  1. 1
    No clear next action

    Launch requirements were scattered across the product.

  2. 2
    Data without context

    New merchants saw dashboard information before it could help them.

  3. 3
    One hierarchy for everyone

    The interface did not adjust as merchant goals changed.

The opportunity was not to build separate dashboards. It was to create one system that changed its priorities as merchant context changed.

Lifecycle stage should determine what comes next

Workshops and affinity mapping organized merchant needs around four recurring jobs. This became the single model used across onboarding, daily operations, and feature discovery.

Activate

Get ready to take the first payment.

Prioritize required tasks, deadlines, installation status, and support.

Configure

Tailor SkyTab to the business.

Sequence setup, teach in context, and confirm readiness.

Operate

Run service with confidence.

Surface performance, system health, exceptions, and timely support.

Optimize

Find opportunities to grow.

Connect trends and merchant needs to relevant capabilities.

Discovery input

The work reframed onboarding as an ongoing lifecycle rather than a single setup flow. Merchant context, not account type, became the strongest organizing principle.

One merchant journey, from setup to growth

The dashboard keeps its structure familiar while changing the information and actions that deserve priority.

Activate and configure

Make the critical path impossible to miss

Required tasks, launch progress, installation details, menu programming, and Customer Success support move to the foreground. Optional features remain available without competing with launch work.

  • Clear ownership and status for every launch requirement
  • Progressive setup that builds confidence
  • Direct access to support when a blocker appears
Operate

Replace setup guidance with useful business context

After launch, the same dashboard shifts toward real-time performance, operational status, tutorials, and recommendations that support the restaurant's daily work.

  • Data appears when it can be interpreted and acted on
  • Exceptions and system health become visible
  • Education continues after initial onboarding
Optimize

Use context to support feature discovery

Relevant capabilities appear as the merchant is ready for them, connecting feature activation to an immediate business need instead of presenting a generic catalog.

Iteration focused the hierarchy on readiness

Explorations compared how setup, status, and optional capabilities should compete for attention. The final direction gave required launch work a clear visual lead.

What I learned

Complexity handled at the system level can create a simpler experience for merchants. A shared structure also made it possible to change priorities without making each lifecycle stage feel like a different product.

What I would test next

I would explore stronger mobile workflows, predictive feature recommendations, and performance benchmarks that help live merchants understand what to do next.
